Face Hugger

I attempt to take in every detail

There is a dip beneath your left eye
Where skin should be
The brows above your eyes
Are thinner on the outside
The inside is more full
More on the right than the left
Four wrinkles lap up your lower lids
Like the tail of a comet, all pointing to the starfall tear ducts
A crook on the bridge of your nose
Attempting to gather all your freckles
There’s less gum over your left canine
What a bad girl she’s been
And the corners on the dip in your chin
Bite back just as hard

Do you mind
How enveloped in you I am?

Gligar, the Fly Scorpion Pokémon

“Gligar do as their name implies; they cannot properly fly, but they do glide. Gligar make their nests at high elevation, usually on the corners of mountainsides or exceptionally tall trees. They jump from there, plummeting from the sky before slowing their descent with their wings, though they never let the wind carry them too far of a distance away from their nest. Because their wings connect to their legs and their tails protrude from lower on their body instead of their lower back, Gligar are unable to walk normally and instead have to hop around on all four when traversing flat land surfaces, though they are able to more naturally climb angled surfaces like trees and mountainsides to return to their nests just fine.”
“This high-altitude lifestyle is specifically a choice for hunting. Gligar sneak up on their prey by dropping on them from the sky, so living in high elevations gives them the time to stalk slowly in the air before diving down. Once they are confident their target does not suspect their approach, Gligar aim for the head. Their legs wrap around their prey’s neck and their clawed hands behind their head, wrapping their wings around their face and ears to all but suffocate their prey; because the goal isn’t to suffocate them, but to distract and blind them from their real plan. When their limbs are secured, Gligar stab the tip of their tail into their prey’s neck, injecting them with a paralytic that will kill most prey in under sixty seconds, or around thirty for especially small prey.”
