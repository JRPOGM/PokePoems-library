Infinity Day

Time is a circle so small
That the rotation is almost unnoticable
The repetition expected without reaction
Revolving doors of returning days
The sun rises and falls
Yet the days never truly end
The calendar does not turn the page
The clock never strikes midnight
There is no new year when it is all more of the same

Hoothoot, the Owl Pokémon

“Hoothoot have an internal organ that can sense the planet’s rotation, giving the Pokémon insight as to when in the day it is, even when they are inside without any other means of judging time and daylight. If they are moved across the world in a short timeframe, by air travel or other, this organ helps the Pokémon acclimate and repattern their behavior to the new time zone. This also helps give the Pokémon an attuned sense of balance and control of their body, timed to the rhythm of the Earth’s rotation. Hoothoot often stand on only one leg at a time, swapping to the other so quickly that it can go by unperceived by others even when they are tired. They will almost never put both feet down at a time, even to brace for an attack they cannot dodge, because their bodies become so accustomed to only using one at a time.”
“This has led to an odd relationship between people and the Hoothoot species, though. Before clocks became a commonplace item worldwide, Hoothoot were one of the few means of telling time many ancient communities relied on, and one of the only organic substitutions to other man-made alternatives. Because of their accuracy in telling the time with the tilt of their bodies and the count of their hoots at timed intervals, some of the oldest civilizations believed Hoothoot to be a legendary species descended from deities with control over the concept of time, like the Sinnoh legend Dialga or the Johto’s fairytales of Celebi; some believed the species to be of a Legendary status itself, and its evolution included. No evidence today supports any connection of the species to either Legendary Pokémon or any other involved with time-related powers, but the mythos still exists today through a smaller pool of believers.”
