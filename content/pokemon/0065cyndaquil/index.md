# Why Fire For Rage

<blockquote>
Is there anger towards the sun<br>
That warms our lives?<br>
Is there fury for the fireplace<br>
Fighting winter on our behalf?<br>
Do we hate the campfire<br>
For its ash interjecting in ghost stories?<br>
Does our blood boil at the stove<br>
As it boils our pasta?<br>
Is our first thought to fight<br>
When it engulfs our homes?

Burning rage cannot match a fire’s combustion<br>
But it is a flame all its own<br>
Red, light and blue, greater than any star<br>
Pumps our hearts fast<br>
Bakes skin warn<br>
And glints eyes with a searing glare

Like every source<br>
Matchstick, furnace, coal or oil<br>
We have something to feel<br>
Towards that burning other

We feel rage foam at the mouth<br>
Anger that breaks skin against wood<br>
Fury that stomps in heavy footsteps

For it is not the fire that ignites itself<br>
And its reach is not in its control<br>
It is ours to simmer<br>
That which threatens to engulf us and each other
</blockquote>

## Cyndaquil, the Fire Mouse Pokémon

![hand-drawn artwork](/images/cyndaquil.jpeg)

> “_Cyndaquil are timid creatures. Despite the musculature and bone structure to support their bodies on their hind legs, this species prefers to move around on all fours, decreasing their height if and when faced by larger predators. They do not directly attempt to fight aggressors or assailants, instead choosing to act solely defensively. On the back of all Cyndaquil are a series of organic ‘exhausts,’ where the Pokémon shoot out fire from a single organ connected to each vent. While they can propel this fire offensively, they mostly ignite it when attackers get too close, holding a prolonged ignition on their backs that ward off others from getting too close and suffering from burns as a result. Though they are small, their firepower is incredibly strong, and can leave lasting burns even on other Pokémon usually more resistant to fire from other species._”

> “_Similar to how the Charmander’s tail flame is indicative of the species’ health, a Cyndaquil’s flame reflects the individual’s emotions. They can ignite suddenly when surprised, angered, afraid or overcome with joy. Their intensity will fluctuate with their moods, dimming when weak or bursting when high-energy. Of all emotions, rage and anger result in the most powerful combustion from a Cyndaquil, capable of rivaling their first evolution Quilava’s firepower on average; if timid or incapable of braving what challenges them, they can struggle to maintain a fire at all. Wildly swapping between emotions can cause physical harm to a Cyndaquil, as the continuous swaps between intensity can overheat its other organs, specifically its heart and lungs, so it is advised any care or confrontation with the species maintain a consistent tone or are kept to short bursts to prevent permanent harm to any individual._”
