# A Woman of Two Faces

<blockquote>
She presents two souls at once<br>
Two spirits inhabiting one<br>
Her green eye shines with fresh tears<br>
While the blow glows under the lamppost with mirth<br>
The left foot twists on the ball of her foot<br>
And the right presses its sole deeper in the dirt<br>
Black and blue speckles each arm<br>
As rosy pink dusts either cheek

A woman with heterochromia eyes<br>
Living two lives<br>
And I will try to be there for both
</blockquote>

## Chinchou, the Angler Pokémon

![hand-drawn artwork](/images/chinchou.jpeg)

> “_Many species of Pokémon live in the darkest depths of the ocean, and there are likely many species still to be discovered -- alive and deceased -- hiding down there; of the species we do know, Chinchou are actually quite abundant and noticeable, thanks to the lights on the end of their antennae. Originally grown from fins that protrude on their upper back, their antennae bulbs are filled with cells that generate strong positive and negative electricity they pass between each other, providing a faint glow of light that illuminates a small portion of their surroundings in the ocean depths. Chinchou also use this electricity to stun other fish Pokémon, intentionally or not, as the energy in their antennae continuously build and withholding too much energy can negatively impact their own health._”

> “_Chinchou can also use the light of their antennae to communicate via a language similar to Morse code. They often only convey simple messages, warning others of their territory when they pass through or of other Pokémon they pass by. Unable to understand their meaning, other species of Pokémon are simply captivated by the light show Chinchou put on, an unconscious tendency the Angler Pokémon use to their advantage to lure in prey; this has also made the species a fan favorite among night time fisherman to use to their own advantage._”
